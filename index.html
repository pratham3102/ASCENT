Altitude Safety & Climber Emergency Notification Technology

Every climb deserves a safe return.

A.S.C.E.N.T. is a wearable safety system for mountain climbers. It tracks health and altitude, links climbing buddies on the trail, and tells base camp the moment something goes wrong.

Project overview

Climbers lose time when a problem can't be reported.

The problem

Altitude illness, harsh weather, no way to call for help

Mountain climbers face serious risk from altitude illness and sudden weather. High in the mountains there is rarely a reliable way to tell anyone what is happening, so rescue starts late.

Our solution

An IoT safety system with three connected parts

A.S.C.E.N.T. is built from a smartwatch worn by the climber, a buddy unit carried by a partner, and a base-camp station with a live dashboard. Together they monitor health, raise alerts, and keep the group in touch.

Monitor

Watch vital health parameters and the surrounding conditions all the way up.

Connect

Keep hikers in touch with a buddy and with base camp in remote areas.

Alert

Raise an alarm fast and give rescuers a clear picture of who needs help.

  • Earlier warning of altitude trouble
  • Faster, better-informed rescue
  • More confidence for climbers and families
  • Dependable group communication

Real case study · Mount Everest, 1996

What the 1996 Everest disaster teaches us

Expedition leader Rob Hall of New Zealand was caught high on the mountain in a blizzard. Exhaustion, frostbite and running out of oxygen made the situation worse, and the group's ability to communicate with the outside world was limited.

A.S.C.E.N.T. is designed for situations like this: continuous monitoring of the climber, buddy alerts, a last-known location, and an SOS that reaches base camp.

How it works

Three devices, one safety net.

The watch and buddy unit send their readings and alerts to a base-camp receiver over Tellemetary communication. The receiver serves the live dashboard that the rescue team watches.

Unit 1 · Wearable

Climber's smartwatch

  • Round 360 × 360 touch display
  • Pulse, oxygen, temperature, pressure, altitude
  • Hold-to-send SOS with a cancel countdown
  • Fall detection and a local alarm

Unit 2 · Buddy communication

Partner's buddy unit

  • Receives the watch's quick messages
  • Raises its own SOS and fall alerts
  • Reports its own environment readings
  • Hold the button for 3 s to toggle SOS

Base camp · Dashboard

Receiver and live dashboard

  • ESP32 server found at ascent.local
  • Vitals, location, alerts and history per traveler
  • Trip assignment: name, destination, days
  • Buzzer on incoming SOS
  1. MonitorSensors read health, motion and environment.
  2. ProcessThe ESP32-S3 checks the readings on the device.
  3. AlertAbnormal values or a fall raise a warning.
  4. SOSThe climber holds SOS; a 3 s countdown allows cancel.
  5. CommunicateLatest data and location go to base camp.
  6. RescueBase camp sees the alert and acts quickly.

Features

Built for gloved hands and thin air.

Health monitoring

Pulse, blood oxygen and a temperature channel, with separate trend graphs on the watch.

Altitude and conditions

Barometric altitude, air temperature, pressure and pressure direction, with ascent and descent per trek.

Hold-to-send SOS

Hold for 1.5 s, then a 3 s countdown lets you cancel a false alarm. SOS stays available even when the screen is locked.

Fall detection

The motion sensor watches for free-fall, impact and rotation, then raises the alert straight away.

Buddy messages

Five one-tap messages: I'm safe, Regroup, Water, Return, Help. Each shows whether it was delivered.

Base-camp dashboard

Live vitals, position, incident log and traveler profile for every device in the field.

Local alarm sounds

Separate SOS and fall sounds play from the watch and repeat until cleared, with a minimum emergency volume.

Trek sessions

Start, pause and finish a trek. Water and rest reminders count only active trekking time.

Trip assignment

Base camp assigns traveler name, destination and trip length, and the watch shows the days left.

Business model · A tech startup for safer summits

From a school project to a product people pay for.

Adventure and trekking tourism keeps growing, and so does the need for safety that travels on the body. A.S.C.E.N.T. gives operators and rescue teams a single system instead of separate gadgets.

  • Value propositionReal-time safety, health monitoring and emergency response in one kit.
  • Where it's differentA three-device network with a buddy link, a sensor set aimed at altitude, and a base-camp dashboard.

Who it's for

Outdoor enthusiasts

Trekkers and climbers who want a safety net on every trip.

Search and rescue teams

Teams that need to know who is in trouble, and where.

Adventure tour operators

Guides responsible for whole groups on high-altitude routes.

Military and defence

Units operating in remote, high terrain.
